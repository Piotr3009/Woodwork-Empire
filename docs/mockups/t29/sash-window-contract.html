Windows and doors as standing contracts (CLAUDE.md T29 2.12)

The offer of a standing contract for sash windows (2.12), drawn by the game's own renderers from companies the engine built: the Orders board's Contracts tab in its folder (renderContracts: the offer tile, and the block of a running contract) and the Work Plan's Contracts tab on its board (renderWorkPlan: the offer card), each beside the same screen as it is today (v83) with a cut sheet pack. The company is in the 800 m² unit with the timber kit of Turn 28 at classes whose places hold its crew, two joiners (Sam, experienced, and Tom, very experienced) and the owner, so the hall line is v83's to the word. Every offer is the game's own draw for its day (drawContract off offerCarrier), on the first working day whose draw gives that piece; its price is contractPriceFor at the reference, without the client's answer. A sash window is 190 minutes of the owner's work and £249 a piece: the experienced joiner at 226 minutes on the timber plan at the standard class, £127 of his time, £22 of the standard four sided planer's wear and £100 of the day's £200 over the two he makes in a day (casement window 179 minutes and £218, French door 214 minutes and £241, two a day each). The client sends the timber and the glass, so nothing comes off the racks and the tile says so in place of the sheets; the offer card has no material row, its wear is the timber machines', or by hand in a hall with none of them, and no machine tip is offered for it. A running timber contract names the timber machines where the saw's sentence was. Last, the name a contract is drawn with, by pluralOf: Drawer boxes, where v83 writes Drawer boxs.
